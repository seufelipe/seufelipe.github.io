Vandy is a fleet management platform built by ZF, mostly focused on small and medium-sized businesses. I worked on it as Product Designer from 2022 to 2026, based in Dublin.

### From the ground up

I joined the team very early. I was First Designer on a team of mostly backend developers.

Worked with an external agency on user research. Together with a Product Owner, I developed and expanded on the initial ideas, based on the findings.

We also developed a set of archetypes and personas. At first, they were based on interviews with real fleet owners: people who could become our customers, but weren't using Vandy yet, simply because there was no Vandy to use. They were all French fleet admins.

Once the app was out and we had real users, the personas grew with the product. We fed usage analytics into the process and used AI to make them more diverse and more realistic:

- fleet admins from more countries, such as Poland and Spain;
- fleet sizes and company structures closer to those of our actual customers;
- a new set of driver personas, for the people on the other side of the app.

### One product, many brands, many languages

Vandy is an international product. It's used across several countries, by drivers and fleet admins who speak different languages. On top of that, it's offered white-label: customers can run it under their own brand, with their own logo and colours.

That's a lot of versions of the same screen. Redesigning it for every brand and every language was never going to work.

### Tokens all the way down

The answer was a design system built on tokens. I worked on it with two front-end engineers, from the first decisions on structure to everyday maintenance.

In Figma, colour, text and spacing all live in variables. A brand is just a set of values for those variables: swap the set and the whole interface follows. A new brand means new values, not new screens.

On top of the tokens sits a library of reusable components, documented in Figma and Storybook. Every component was designed to work in any language and look right in any brand's colours.

### A little help from AI

White-label customers arrive with a logo and brand colours, and we need to turn that into a full palette for the interface. Brand colours don't always pass accessibility contrast checks, so it's not as simple as copying them over.

So I built a small internal tool with Claude Code. It takes a customer's logo and generates a colour palette that meets WCAG contrast requirements. Onboarding a new brand got a lot quicker.

AI ended up in other parts of the design system work too:

- documenting component states and edge cases, which is important but slow to do by hand;
- producing illustrations;
- working on user stories in Claude Code, producing prototypes and spec files, then using the Figma MCP server to turn them into screens in Figma.
